## The Story and Growth of Wycliffe Africa

The story of Wycliffe Africa began in the late 1990s with a conversation between Edwyn Kiptinness and John Bendor-Samuel around a growing need within the Bible translation movement: raising Africans as missionaries to participate in and support Bible translation in Africa.

At the time, there was an increasing desire among African communities and churches to see Africans actively involved in the work of Bible translation. This was informed by the declining number of Western Missionaries who had been at the frontline in serving in the Bible Translation movement. This created both a challenge and an opportunity - the need to intentionally raise and equip African missionaries who could serve within the Bible translation movement across the continent.

It was from this vision that Wycliffe Africa was born.

Wycliffe Africa was eventually registered in 2004 in Nairobi, Kenya, with an intentional African mandate: to recruit, train, send and support African missionaries who would serve Bible translation organizations across Africa, particularly within the Wycliffe Global Alliance Africa Area and SIL Africa Area.

From the beginning, the vision was ambitious. Wycliffe Africa set out to raise at least 55 missionaries within approximately two years. By God's grace, that target was achieved.

As the movement continued to grow, some of the missionaries and structures that had initially been part of Wycliffe Africa developed into independent national entities. This included the establishment of Wycliffe Ethiopia, Wycliffe Togo and Wycliffe Benin. Consequently, the number of missionaries directly under Wycliffe Africa reduced for a season, but the vision did not diminish. Instead, the work continued to grow and evolve.

Today, Wycliffe Africa has 31 missionaries serving across various African countries, including Madagascar, South Africa, South Sudan, Kenya, Nigeria, Uganda, The Gambia, Cameroon, Ghana, Tunisia, Tanzania and Eurasia with the number continuing to grow.

Our missionaries serve in a variety of critical areas that strengthen Bible translation movements across the continent. Their roles include:

- Bible translation consultancy
- Human resources and senior administration
- Scripture engagement
- Language technologies
- Language programme management

As Bible translation continues to embrace new technologies, the opportunities and needs are also changing. Technology is enabling translation teams to draft Scripture more quickly and efficiently. However, faster translation also creates an increasing need for skilled Bible translation consultants and other specialists who can help ensure that translated Scripture is accurate, clear, natural and meaningful to the communities receiving it.

Wycliffe Africa therefore looks to the future with faith and expectation.

The vision that began with a conversation between two African leaders continues today: to see more Africans raised, equipped and sent to serve the Bible translation movement.

Our desire is to see a growing number of African missionaries serving wherever they are needed, across Africa and beyond, providing the skills, leadership and spiritual commitment required for Bible translation to flourish.

We believe the future of Bible translation in Africa requires African participation at every level. And as God continues to call and equip people, Wycliffe Africa is committed to raising more missionaries for the work, so that more communities can have access to God's Word in languages they understand.
